Individual 95% intervals do not provide joint 95% coverage. Non-significance does not establish equivalence or safety. Amounts are fictional USD; after-refund revenue is not profit.

02

More buyers, nearly flat revenue estimate.

Gross revenue per assigned user is -$0.06 at the point estimate. Its 95% interval runs from -$1.45 to $1.27, so these data do not establish either a gain or a loss. Gross revenue equals conversion × orders per buyer × average order value. In this sample, lower repeat ordering and basket value offset the conversion contribution. Why use a user bootstrap for revenue?

Approximately 87% of control users and 87% of new-checkout users generate zero revenue. The top 1% of assigned users account for 26.3% and 25.1% of gross revenue, respectively.

We resample assigned users within each arm 10,000 times, retaining zeroes and all orders and refunds for each selected user. No outliers are deleted. A separate normal-approximation interval gives a similar conclusion: -$1.56 to $1.00 for after-refund revenue/user.

03

Validate assignment before interpreting effects.

No allocation alert at p < 0.001. There are 24 exact ingestion retries and 0 conflicting assignments. The pipeline rejects conflicting assignments, broken keys, malformed timestamps, excess refunds and immature follow-up. SQL has its own assignment and maturity gates.

Every assigned user reconciles

Independent Python logic reproduces the SQL order, refund and checkout outcomes for all 24,000 users. 179 orders are excluded by the measurement contract: 40 not paid, 25 before assignment, and 114 after the purchase window.

An unflagged allocation test does not prove exposure delivery or tracking are correct. The simulation assumes complete identities and logs.

The limiting factor is revenue precision.

A $0.25 loss limit requires about 644,548 users per group under zero true revenue loss. At 50,000 per group, power is only 12%. A bigger conversion test alone does not solve the launch decision.

How sensitive is this to business assumptions?

Under zero true revenue change, a larger acceptable-loss margin requires less traffic. These are planning scenarios, not suggested threshold changes.

If the observed revenue estimate of -$0.28 were the true effect, it would fall below the -$0.25 safety boundary; more users would not make that effect safe. If the observed refund increase persisted, refund safety alone would need about 278,063 users per arm. Pilot effects are uncertain, so these are scenarios, not predictions.

  1. Agree on economics. Define acceptable loss using margin, implementation cost and customer impact; confirm available traffic.
  2. Decide whether confirmation is worthwhile. Estimate test cost and precision for all criteria. If infeasible, retain the current checkout and redesign.
  3. Predefine the next test. Use a fresh independent fixed-horizon run, or a valid prospective sequential design. Specify sample cap and mature follow-up.
  4. Launch only after all agreed criteria clear. Operational readiness still requires review, followed by a staged rollout.
05

What a reviewer should challenge

Simulation and external validity

All records are fictional and the randomization process is disclosed. The observed recommendation is based on estimated effects, not the simulator's hidden probabilities. Real experiments require audits of identities, assignment delivery, payment reconciliation, tracking gaps and spillovers.

Repeated checking and subgroup fishing

Stopping at the first favorable ordinary confidence interval increases false positives. Use a fixed horizon or a valid sequential design. Searching many device, customer or date slices creates accidental winners. This project makes no segment launch claims; balance and shorter-window diagnostics are exploratory.

Refund interpretation and missing costs

Refunds among buyers are 9.66% versus 10.82%, but purchasing is affected by assignment. That conditional comparison is descriptive. The primary refund guardrail retains all assigned users. Neither revenue metric includes fees, fulfillment costs, profit margin, chargebacks or long-term customer effects.
